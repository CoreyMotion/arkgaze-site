The day

Six hours. Nothing generic on the screen.

Every exercise runs on your team's real inbox, documents and spreadsheets. Every module ends with something you keep.

9:00 – 9:30

Reset the mental model

What these tools are and aren't: a brilliant, fast, over-confident intern with no memory. Where they shine, where they invent things, and why "it was wrong once" is a reason to learn verification, not to give up.

9:30 – 10:00

Safe use and your data rules

What can and can't be pasted, client confidentiality, the Privacy Act basics that apply to an office, workspace vs personal accounts, turning training-data off. The team edits a pre-filled policy together and the manager signs it off before morning tea.

You keep: a signed AI Safe Use Policy
10:00 – 11:00

Prompting that actually works

One framework, drilled on real work: role, ask, context, example, then iterate. Asking for options, asking it to critique its own draft, asking it what it needs to know. Each person rewrites a real email and a real document from their own week.

You keep: 3 personal prompts each
11:00 – 11:15

Break

11:15 – 12:30

The daily toolkit lab

Six stations, each person picks two: email and writing, meetings-to-minutes-to-actions, documents and proposals, spreadsheets, reading long PDFs and contracts (with a verification step), and customer replies in your tone. Each station ends with a reusable prompt saved to the shared library.

You keep: a shared prompt library
12:30 – 1:00

Lunch

1:00 – 2:00

Build your own assistant

Load your tone guide, templates and SOPs into a persistent assistant so nobody re-explains the business every morning. Teams build one each — proposal writer, customer reply desk, onboarding guide — and test it on today's work.

You keep: 2–3 working team assistants
2:00 – 3:00

Automation Opportunity Map

Your recurring processes go up on the wall and get scored by frequency, minutes and pain. Then we sort them: keep manual, AI-assisted, or automate outright — with a live demo of a real automation running end to end.

You keep: a ranked list with hours saved per item
3:00 – 3:30

Your 30-day plan

Each person commits to three uses. The team picks one shared assistant to adopt. The manager gets a one-page measurement sheet, and the check-in call goes in the calendar before anyone leaves.

You keep: a 30-day plan and a check-in date

Questions

The things people ask on the call

Which AI tools do you teach?

Whatever your office already pays for. Microsoft 365 shops train on Copilot alongside ChatGPT or Claude; Google shops on Gemini alongside one of the same two. The method is the same across all of them, so nothing you learn is wasted when a vendor renames a feature.

Is our client data safe?

That's the second module of the day, and it's why you leave with a signed policy and the account settings fixed. Most offices already have the risk and no policy; the workshop closes it. We never retain client documents used in exercises.

Our staff are sceptical — or have tried it and it got things wrong.

Good. It will get things wrong again, which is why verification is taught as a skill, not a warning. The sceptic's most dreaded task becomes the first live example of the day. A converted sceptic is the best result we get.

Do we need to buy licences first?

No. Free tiers are enough for the day, and we'll tell you on the call which paid plan, if any, is worth it for your team — we don't sell licences.

How many people, and where?

Up to 12 in one room, at your office, anywhere in Auckland. Larger teams run as two cohorts. Bring laptops and access to your own email and documents; we bring everything else, including a backup internet connection.

Who runs it?

Corey from Arkgaze — a practitioner who builds automations and AI systems for New Zealand businesses, not a presenter working from a slide deck. Every example on the screen is from a real office.

What happens after the day?

Within three working days you get the written report: what was covered, the shared prompt library, the team assistants and who owns each, and the Automation Opportunity Map with a fixed-price quote for the top items. The quote is there if you want it; the workshop stands on its own either way.
